# **Step by Step**

In order to create a character, you must first know what kind of person you want to be within the story. Are you a stoic warrior with a heart of gold? A greedy mage in search of the most powerful spells? Or perhaps a corrupt priest who simply wishes to find the best alcohol in the world?

In exactly what order you build your character in is your choice, but this is a list of things you will need to play Journey’s End (and also the order in which the book details them):

1. **Class(es):** Every character has skill points in at least one class, which will define or shape the abilities of what you can do.  
2. **Race:** Choose between the innovative humans, long-living elves, or the hearty dwarves\!  
3. **Traits:** Determine what your character is good at, from lifting heavy objects to beguiling others.  
4. **Experiences:** Write out some background information that influences more specific skills your character excels in.  
5. **Equipment:** Choose items for your character to start their journey with, from axes to staffs to absolutely nothing if you so wish.

# **Choose a Class**

Your class is a defining feature of your character, determining whether you wield magic or weapons and how you acquire your abilities. While each class is better suited to certain roles, such as the Priest excelling in healing, every class is capable of being built in many different ways.

## **Class Options**

Below is a list of classes that are available to choose from. More details regarding each class can be found in a later section of the book.

Each class comes with two **subclasses** that provide more distinction of your character. When you choose a class, you will immediately choose a subclass.

### **Hero**

Heroes are those who stand up for their values with great courage. Often guided by an unwavering sense of justice, they are a class of charismatic individuals that serve as both agile fighters and moral support.

**Inspiring.** A beacon of hope or triumph to those who see you, you excel at bringing out the best in others.

**Paragon.** An agile warrior feared by those who stand against your values, you strike faster than the eye can see.

### **Mage**

Mages study for years to develop their mana to cast spells. While their strength lies in training and inner nature, it all hinges on their ability to visualize the cause and effects of the spell. Mages are often educated individuals who offer both utility and raw power.

**Studied.** One who has dedicated many hours to learning and understanding magic, you have a vast amount of spells at your disposal.

**Instinctual.** One who has focused entirely on the visualization of your magic, you have a talent for augmenting spells.

### **Monk**

Monks are disciplined in both body and mind. Often pious towards the Goddess of Creation, they undergo harsh training to survive on little and rely only on their bodies to defend themselves. They are a self-reliant class with very few direct weaknesses.

**Wise.** You have learned more than a few hard lessons in your life, allowing you to guide others on their journey.

**Martial.** You have learned to wield discipline through physical force, using your own body to strike back at those who would do you harm.

### **Priest**

Priests are holy spellcasters born with the gift to wield the Magic of the Goddess. Often becoming a member of the clergy, they focus on supporting their allies through healing and buffs. However, they are still very capable of doing harm.

**Healer.** Your talents have led you to excel at alleviating injuries and ailments from others, making sure everyone stays alive on their journey.

**Enchanter.** Your talents focus on bolstering those around you, ensuring that they fight stronger than ever.

### **Thief**

Thieves are sneaky individuals who exploit weaknesses and tend to avoid direct confrontation. Their strength lies in their cunning and resourcefulness, finding ways to escape from any bad situation.

**Delver.** Your time robbing homes or plundering dungeons has honed your senses, allowing you to detect traps and hidden threats.  
**Connected.** Your time interacting with others has built a large web of contacts, allowing you to call on their aid when necessary.

### **Warrior**

Warriors are trained fighters who specialize in close-quarters combat and defensive maneuvers. They are able to perform superhuman displays of strength and resilience, allowing them to serve as frontliners to their allies.

**Tough.** You are trained to resist all threats, physical or magical, so that you may take the pain that others can not.

**Brutal.** You are trained to inflict unbelievable damage upon your targets, slaying them before they can do the same to you.

## **Multiclassing**

While all adventurers have a class, some may exhibit traits from multiple classes. To do so costs **Skill Points**, which will be detailed later in the **Assigning Traits** section (and also the **Rules of Play** section).

# **Choose a Race**

Your race is a marker of your character’s heritage and natural abilities. Each race comes with a unique feature that influences their abilities.

While the series has only shown humans, elves, and dwarves, you can use those as a guide to implement your own fantastical people\!

## **Race Options**

Below is a list of races that are available to choose from. More details regarding each race can be found in a later section of the book. 

### **Human**

Humans are short-lived but highly innovative and curious people. They are highly sociable, compensating for their short lifespans by passing on their knowledge to the next generation. As a result, their species has advanced rapidly in fields like medicine, engineering, and magic.

Human adults often reach heights between 4 and 7 feet tall, and live for up to a century.

### **Elf**

Elves are a rare, often isolated people who are capable of living for thousands of years. To them, months could feel like a mere day. Elves tend to have difficulty with boredom and feel little urgency outside of life-threatening events. Being living witnesses to history, they are often well learned and well travelled. 

Elven adults often grow to be between 4 and 5 feet tall, and live for so long that many could consider them ageless.

### **Dwarf**

Dwarves are naturally strong and durable people, often possessing talents in physically demanding professions. Dwarven culture often pushes them to master a single skill like blacksmithing or combat. Though they live for centuries, many still seek companionship with humans and their own kind.

Dwarven adults often grow to be between 3 and 4 feet tall and live for about 4 centuries.

# **Assigning Traits**

When creating a character, you’ll need to determine the basic skills and traits your character is good at. These are done through a resource called **Skill Points**.

## **Skill Points**

Journey’s End uses a Skill Point system rather than a levelling system to allow greater customization.  
Skill points can be used for any of the following:

* Improving any of the Basic Traits  
* Improving any of your Class Traits  
* Improving any of your Class Tiers  
* Improving any of your Experiences  
* Learning new techniques or spells  
* Improving any of your techniques or spells  
* Taking an additional Class

When you’re creating a character, you start out with 40 **Skill Points**. However, you must spend a certain number of **Skill Points** in the following ways:

* At least 18 **Skill Points** on **Basic Traits**  
* At least 9 **Skill Points** on **Class Traits**  
* At least 8 **Skill Points** on **Spells or Techniques**

The last 5 **Skill Points** may be spent in any of the 3 categories above.

## **Tiers**

Classes, traits, spells, and techniques all have a **Tier**, which represents how proficient you are in that particular field. When you first create a character, they all start at **Tier 1**. Whenever you improve a trait, spell, or technique with **Skill Points**, its **Tier** increases by 1\. The cost to improve a trait, spell, or technique equals its current **Tier.**

## **Basic Traits**

There are six Basic Traits, also known as Stats: 

* Agility  
* Charisma  
* Dexterity  
* Instinct  
* Knowledge  
* Strength

Each Basic Trait represents a different type of natural or trained ability. All Basic Traits have a modifier of \-1 at **Tier 1\.**

## **Class Traits**

Class Traits are listed in your class description and include traits such as Health, Stamina, and Mana. Unlike Basic Traits, Class Traits may begin at a value specified by your class.

Your class also denotes your **Primary Trait**, which is the trait most associated with what your class excels in. If your Spells or Techniques require a **check,** it will be made using your **Primary Trait.**

## **Class Tiers**

Class Tiers represents how skilled you are in a given class. Reaching higher Class Tiers grants you additional Class Features or Subclass Features. Class Tiers are unique to each class you have skill points in.

Unlike other improvable traits, Class Tiers cost more skill points. The cost to improve a Class Tier is 5 × its current **Tier**.

### **High Tier and Total Tier**

Some rules refer to your **Total Tier** or **High Tier**. Your **Total Tier** is the combined total of all your class Tiers. Your **High Tier** is the highest Tier you have in a single class. 

## **Experiences**

Experiences are lessons or skills your character has learned throughout their life before adventuring. They are boiled down to a single word or short phrase that encompasses the experience.

Each character starts with two Experiences. You should come up with a short list of possible Experiences based on your character’s backstory and work with your GM (or even other players) to determine which ones to choose and in what kind of scenarios they may be used in.  
While there is no defined list of Experiences, the following is a list of examples:

* **Backgrounds:** Blacksmith, Bouncer, Teacher, Scholar, Acrobat, Soldier, Criminal, Town Guard, Jester, Musician  
* **Characteristics:** Stone-faced, Observant, Charming, Intimidating, Silver-Tongued  
* **Specialities:** Mediator, Poison Expert, Baffling Strength, Climber  
* **Phrases:** Now You See Me, Take it or Leave it, By Your Side, Look at Me, It's You and Me.

When your character makes a trait check, you may apply any Experience that fits the given situation. All Experiences have a modifier of \+2 at **Tier 1**.

## **Learning Techniques and Spells**

Techniques and Spells are specific actions your character can take during their journey. Each class has a specific list of techniques or spells to choose from. 

All Techniques and Spells have a Tier requirement, which is displayed on their cards. If your Class Tier is not equal to or higher than the Tier requirement, you can not learn it.

Learning Techniques and Spells costs Skill Points, which is displayed on their cards.

## **Improving Techniques and Spells**

Like improving Experiences or Class Traits, Techniques and Spells can be improved by spending Skill Points. Each Technique and Spell begins at Tier 1\.

Improving Techniques and Spells enables you to augment them further, which will be described further in the upcoming **Rules of Play** sections.

## **Taking Another Class (Multiclassing)**

As stated before, some characters can have Skill Points in multiple classes. Doing so represents spending time and effort to be proficient in multiple fields.

Gaining a new class that is not your first costs 10 **Skill Points**. You gain the class’s **Tier 1** features and may begin learning **Spells** or **Techniques** from that class. You also gain its **Class Traits**. However, you do not replace any **class traits** you have already received from another **class**.

For example, if your original class is a Mage and you multiclass into a Priest, both classes share the Mana **Class Trait**. In this case, you keep the value and Tier of your Mana **Class Trait** from the Mage.

The only excepton to this is your **Primary Trait**. If you gain another class that uses a different **Primary Trait**, you use the second **Primary Trait** for spells or techniques from the second class.

# **Equipment**

Depending on the type of adventure your character is playing in and how far along their journey they already are, they may have more or less equipment than what is described here.

## **Choosing Starting Equipment**

Depending on your original class, you will have a list of choices for the equipment you start out with. Generally, they will include the basic necessities for your class to function (holy scriptures for a priest or a weapon for a warrior).

You’ll also have the choice for several “general” items, such as healing potions or rope that could aid you on your journey.

## **Starting Magical Items**

If your GM allows it, you may choose to have a magical item that your character managed to acquire before the journey.

In the dedicated **Equipment** section, you’ll find a table of basic magical items that are meant for new adventurers. If your character is starting out more experienced, you may be allowed to choose from more advanced tables.

# **Backstory**

Every adventurer has a story prior to the journey. Who are you? Why do you adventure? What do you believe in?

Beyond that, however, is how do you relate or know your fellow adventurers? If you knew any of them before your first session, you should talk with the other player and determine what you know and think of each other.

Your character’s backstory is deeply intertwined with their **Experiences**. If you have already written your character’s **Experiences,** you should base your backstory around them and vice versa.

## **Backstory Probing Questions**

If you are having trouble or want a starting point for your character’s backstory, here are some questions to get you going\!

If you want to select some randomly, you can roll a die displayed in the table’s header.

**Backstory Questions Table (d10)**

| What is an ideal or moral that you will (or hope to) always abide by? |
| :---- |
| When you were at your lowest, who carried you back to safety? Was it your family, a friend, or your own will? |
| Choose an **Experience** from your character sheet. What is the story behind it? |
| What has caused you to venture so far from home (physically and/or metaphorically)? |
| What is your character’s favorite food? Favorite drink? |
| Someone desperate corners you in the alleyway and demands that you give them money or food. How do you handle the situation? |
| If you saw yourself in the mirror, what is the first thought that pops into your head? |
| Do you believe in the Goddess of Creation? What led you to have or not have faith? |
| What is a behavior, big or small, that is unique to you? |
| In what conditions did you live in as you grew up? Do you hail from a wealthy family, a criminal gang, or a simple, humble home? |
