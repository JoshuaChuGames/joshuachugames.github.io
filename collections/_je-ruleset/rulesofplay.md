# Rules of Play {#rules-of-play}

In this section, you’ll learn the general flow of the game, along with core mechanics and how to play the game.

# Flow of the Game {#flow-of-the-game}

In Journey’s End, gameplay is separated into sessions, which is just any time you and your friends gather to play. The structure of any given session can vary, so the Flow of the Game is more of a general pathway for a section of a session.
<div class="break"></div>

Throughout the game, the GM will be describing the world, environment, and people around the players. Meanwhile, the players are stating their actions, thoughts, and words to react to what the GM has described. Finally, the GM determines the consequences and outcomes of the player’s actions (often time with a roll to determine success/failure).
<div class="break"></div>

This loop can be simplified into the following steps:

1. The GM describes the environment  
2. The players and GM ask questions  
3. The players and GM choose actions to take  
4. The GM determines the results of the actions  
5. Repeat

## 1\. The GM Describes the Environment

The GM lays out the environment, dangers, and people surrounding the player characters. This could be stating how tall a building is, the sly smile of the merchant, or a large ball rolling towards the players.

## 2\. The Players and GM Ask Questions

The players and GM ask questions to paint a clearer picture of the environment. These could be whether a player already has their sword drawn or how far away an enemy is.

## 3\. The Players and GM Choose Actions

The players and the GM determine what their characters will do in response to the environment. These could include attacks against an enemy, smooth-talking an angry guard, or investigating a room for clues.
<div class="break"></div>

These almost always require the one performing the action to make a **check**, which is described in an upcoming section.

## 4\. The GM Determines the Results

Depending on the outcome of any **checks** and if they pass a **difficulty class**, the GM narrates what happens because of it. The GM and players then make changes to any resources that were used or taken during the action.

## 5\. Repeat

The game loop then repeats with the GM describing how the changes affect the environment around the players. Enough of these loops eventually create a cohesive story\!

# Core Mechanics {#core-mechanics}

This section describes the core mechanics of Journey’s End that apply to all characters.

## Focus

Whenever a character, whether it be a player or a non-player character (NPC), is the primary focus of the scene, they have the **Focus** of the game. You can think of it as if the game were a movie or TV show. Whoever the camera is turned to has the **Focus**.
<div class="break"></div>

In certain cases, multiple characters could have the focus, though this is usually reserved for conversations. As soon as any of those characters takes an action, they take the **Focus.**
<div class="break"></div>

There is no specific order to who has the **Focus**, but the general rule of thumb is for every player to have a chance with the **Focus**. Also, the GM should be allowed to take the **Focus** whenever another character in the scene is acting.

### Having the Focus

When you have the **Focus**, you can essentially treat it is as your “turn.” There is no set amount of actions you can take while you have the **Focus**, but you should follow the flow of the narrative and grant others a chance to take the **Focus** when it makes sense. Sometimes, such as when your character is having a triumphant moment in their journey, it makes sense to take the **Focus** for an extended period of time. 

### Taking Actions

When you have the **Focus**, you will take **Actions**, which is anything you perform utilizing your skills, class features, techniques, or spells. Some examples are: casting a spell, making an attack, hiding in a corner, persuading a merchant to give you a discount, or climbing over a ledge.
<div class="break"></div>

Oftentimes, these **Actions** will force you to make a **Check**, which results in a success or a failure. Succeeding a **Check** means being able to perform what you set out to accomplish, while failing a check means the opposite.
<div class="break"></div>

When you take an **Action** but fail the **Check**, that can signal the GM to take the **Focus**.

### GM Focus

When the GM has the Focus, it often means that an NPC or the environment is going to take their own **Actions** to affect the players. Similarly to a player’s **Action**, most GM **Actions** require a **Check.**
<div class="break"></div>

The GM is able to take the **Focus** whenever they feel the story calls for it. In most cases, however, it will be for one of the following reasons:

* A player failed a **Check**  
* The players have spent enough time without taking any **Actions**  
* The GM spends **Doom** to interrupt the players.  
* A new enemy, character, or shift in the environment makes a sudden appearance

The GM usually dictates when the **Focus** falls back on the players, but they should strike a balance so the players do not feel impotent.

### Focus in Combat

Because Journey’s End doesn’t have definitive turns, the flow of combat is largely dictated exactly the same. However, the GM and the players should be extra vigilant in making sure the **Focus** is passed around evenly, so everyone has a chance to impact the battle.

## Dice

Whenever you must determine if you can break down a door, how much damage you deal to an enemy, or how convincing of a lie you speak, you will need to roll dice.
<div class="break"></div>

Technically, you can use any system that can produce random numbers within a range, but traditionally, you would use the following:

* A twenty-sided die (d20)  
* A twelve-sided die (d12)  
* A ten-sided die (d10)  
* A ten-sided percentile die  
* An eight-sided die (d8)  
* A six-sided die (d6)  
* A four-sided die (d4)

Notice that after each die is a shortened moniker. Throughout this rulebook, we will be referring to these dice using the following system:  
**N d S**  
Where N is a number noting how many of these dice must be rolled, and S is the number of sides the die has. For instance: 3d6 means rolling three six-sided dice and summing the total.
<div class="break"></div>

A unique case is the d100, which actually rolls two dice: the d10 and the percentile die (also has ten sides, but its values are 00, 10, 20, etc.). To roll a d100 means rolling both and using the percentile die as the 10s place and the d10 as the 1s place. To roll a 100 on the d100, you must roll a 00 on the percentile die and a 0 on the d10.

## Checks

When you take an **Action**, it will always force you to make what is called a **Check**. **Checks** are essentially a test of your character’s capabilities combined with luck. Whenever you make a **Check**, you must roll a d20 and add any appropriate modifiers to the roll.  
Certain techniques, spells, and features may allow you to add additional modifiers to your roll.
<div class="break"></div>

Every **check** has what is called a **Difficulty Class (DC)**, which is a set value to determine how difficult your action is. In most cases, the GM determines what the **DC** of a **check** is, with some exceptions such as attacks. The goal of any **check** is to have a final value equal to or greater than the **DC**.

### Assigning a Basic Trait

**Checks** will usually have an associated Basic Trait, depending on what you are attempting to do. The GM will indicate this by telling you to “make a \[Basic Trait\] check.” The following is a brief description of each Basic Trait and what it correlates to.

#### Agility

A high Agility indicates a character who is both quick and nimble. You’ll make an Agility check to cover distances quickly, leap across a chasm, or navigate difficult terrain.

#### Charisma

A high Charisma indicates a character who is adept at navigating social situations. You’ll make a Charisma check to lie, convince, or intimidate others.

#### Dexterity

A high Dexterity indicates a character who has precise control over their limbs and movements. You’ll make a Dexterity Check to operate precise tools, shoot weapons, or remain unnoticed.

#### Instinct

A high Instinct indicates a character who is well aware of their surroundings. You’ll make an Instinct check to search, detect lies, or navigate the world around you. 

#### Knowledge

A high Knowledge indicates a character who is well-studied and keen-minded. You’ll make a Knowledge check to recall lore, decipher code, or reconstruct a scene from clues.

#### Strength

A high Strength indicates a character who has physical prowess. You’ll make a Strength check to break objects, lift or push heavy objects, or climb up cliffs.

### Contested Checks

Sometimes, a **check** may be countered by an opposing force, which creates a **contested check**. Two warriors may grapple with each other in a contest of brute strength, and a thief may lie to a skeptical monk. In all cases, the opposing force must be consciously attempting to oppose it. If the character is not expecting it or can not react to it, then the **check** is made against a static **DC**.
<div class="break"></div>

The static **DC** is determined by the modifier that would have been used in a contested check: 10 \+ the appropriate modifier.

### Succeeding a Check

Succeeding on a **check** means that your final value of your roll meets or exceeds the **DC**. This means that whatever your character was attempting to accomplish is successful.

#### Critical Success

A **critical success** is when you roll a 20 on your d20 before adding any modifiers. For nearly all **checks,** this means that you will immediately succeed. The exception is if the **GM** rules the **check** to be impossible for your character to beat, such as a warrior attempting to jump off a cliff and fly. In that case, the outcome of the check will reflect the best feasible outcome, such as a sudden wind pushing the warrior to safety.
<div class="break"></div>

When you roll a **critical success**, you immediately gain a **Miracle**, which will be described later in this section.
<div class="break"></div>

When you roll a **critical success** on an attack, you deal extra damage as described later in this section.

### Failing a Check

Failing on a **check** means that your final value of your roll falls below the **DC**. This means that whatever your character was attempting to accomplish fails.
<div class="break"></div>

After a failure, the GM gains a **Doom**, which will be described later in this section, and may take the **Focus** to alter the environment.

#### Critical Failure

A **critical failure** is when you roll a 1 on your d20 before adding any modifiers. For nearly all **checks,** this means that you will immediately fail. 
<div class="break"></div>

When you roll a **critical failure**, the GM immediately gains a **Doom** on top of the one gained by failing. The only exception is if the GM rolls a **critical failure**.

### Advantage and Disadvantage

Sometimes a **check** is modified by special circumstances to create an advantage or disadvantage.
<div class="break"></div>

A **check** with advantage reflects that something has made your **check** significantly easier. You roll a second d20 when you make the roll and take the higher of the two.
<div class="break"></div>

A **check** with disadvantage reflects that something has made your **check** significantly harder. You roll a second d20 when you make the roll and take the lower of the two.

## Attacks

Whether by swinging a sword or shooting a beam of magic, most attempts to harm another creature require an **attack check**. All **attack checks** use a modifier, either the weapon’s **attack trait** or your **spell/technique trait** if the **attack** is a spell or technique.  
The DC of the **attack check** is always the targeted creature’s **Evasion**.

## Evasion

**Evasion** represents a character’s ability to dodge and weave incoming danger. Your character’s **Evasion** is determined by your Class, but it can be modified by techniques, spells, equipment, and more.

## Damage

When you succeed on an attack check against an enemy, you will roll dice to determine how much damage your attack deals, and thus how many it Points your target loses.

### Spell or Technique Damage Dice

If the attack is made by a spell or technique, the text tells you which damage dice you should roll.

### Weapon Damage Dice

If the attack is made with a weapon, you roll your weapon’s damage die and add the appropriate modifier to the total. You roll a number of weapon damage dice equal to half your **High Tier.**

### Unarmed Damage Dice

When you make an **attack check** without a weapon, you may choose the primary trait to be Strength or Dexterity. It deals 1d6 \+ the chosen Primary Trait damage.

### Critical Successes and Damage

If your attack roll is a **critical success**, your attack roll deals double damage\! Make the damage roll as usual, adding all modifiers, and double the total.

### Multiple Sources of Damage

If damage is dealt more than once to a creature during a character’s **Action**, the damage is always totaled before the enemy loses Hit Points. 

## Distance, Range, and Movement

Journey’s End is made to be playable with the theater of the mind, otherwise known as your imagination. However, using a map can be useful to better showcase an environment or clarify positioning.

### Using a Map

Especially for combat, a map is useful to ensure everyone can see the same battlefield. It is recommended to use a square or hex grid to measure distances. Each grid space should represent roughly a 5 ft. diameter.

### Range

Journey’s End measures distance by a **range**. While these are not precise measurements, there is a quick guide for the GM to determine ranges and distances during play. Each type of **range** is a semi-abstract distance to aid with everyone’s mental image.

* **Melee:** Within touching distance of the target, meaning about a few feet away from a character.  
* **Very Close:** Able to see the target in fine detail and easily able to enter **melee** range, meaning about 5-10 ft. away from a character.  
* **Close:** Able to see the target in detail and can reach **melee** range within seconds, meaning about 10-30 ft. away from a character.  
* **Far:** Able to see the creature but can’t reach them without great speed, meaning about 30-100 ft. away from a character. Without any abilities, the GM might call for an **Agility check** to get within **melee** range of a **far** target.  
* **Very Far**: So far that only the general shape can be seen, meaning about 100-300 ft. away from a character. Without any abilities, the GM might call for an **Agility check** to get within **melee** range of a **very far** target.  
* **Out of Range:** So far that they may not even be visible anymore. Without first moving into a closer range, they usually can not be targeted.

### Using Range

Range refers to an ability’s maximum range. For instance, a spell with a range of **close** means it can target creatures within **melee, very close,** and **close range.**

### Moving

When you take an **action,** you can easily move to a location within **close range** as part of the same **action.** To get farther, however, the GM may call for an **agility check** to get there in time.

## Cover and Line of Sight

Almost all abilities that target a creature require line of sight. Without it, the creature can not be targeted, unless otherwise stated. Similarly, if the target is fully behind cover, such as a wall, then they also can not be targeted.
<div class="break"></div>

If the creature takes cover behind something that would not provide full protection, then attack rolls against them have disadvantage.
<div class="break"></div>

If the target is obscured by darkness, then the character does not have line of sight on them, unless they can see in the dark.

## Concentration

Some spells and techniques require concentration on their effects to maintain, which is denoted on their cards. You lose concentration on any effects when you take **Major** damage or greater.
<div class="break"></div>

You can concentrate on multiple effects at once.

# Core Resources {#core-resources}

This section describes the core resources that all characters possess.

## Hit Points and Damage Thresholds

**Hit Points (HP)** represent the physical and mental injuries your character experiences during their journey. The amount of **Hit Points** you have is determined by your class. When you take damage, you lose a number of Hit Points according to your **damage thresholds.**
<div class="break"></div>

Your **damage thresholds** are determined by your starting class, but can be modified by techniques, spells, equipment, and more. The threshold bar in your character sheet display the values for your **Minor**, **Moderate**, and **Major** thresholds.

### Increasing Damage Thresholds

Oftentimes, abilities, armor, or other equipment will increase your damage thresholds using the following pattern: **\+X/Y/Z**. Where X, Y, and Z represent bonuses to your **Minor**, **Moderate**, and **Major** damage thresholds, respectively.

### Losing Hit Points

When your character takes damage, compare the damage total to your thresholds and mark a number of Hit Points determined by the threshold:

* **Minor damage** is anything above your **Minor** threshold but not above your **Moderate** threshold; you lose 1 HP.  
* **Moderate damage** is anything above your **Moderate** threshold but not above your  **Major** threshold; you lose 2 HP.  
* **Major damage** is anything above your **Major** threshold but not above double your **Major** threshold; you lose 3 HP.

If you ever reduce incoming damage to 0 or less, you don’t lose any Hit Points. 
<div class="break"></div>

If you take damage that is greater than double your **Major** threshold, start with a value equal to your **Major** threshold. Keep doubling that value until it exceeds the damage taken. You lose an additional Hit Point for each time the value was doubled.

### Regaining Hit Points

You usually regain Hit Points through **Resting** or a technique or spell that heals.

## Stamina

**Stamina** represents physical and mental stress your character experiences during their journey. The amount of **Stamina** you have is determined by your class. Some of your abilities might require you to spend **Stamina** to use them. Additionally, the GM might offer you the chance to spend  **Stamina** to succeed in a **check** that you failed or force you to spend **Stamina** as a consequence of a failed **check**.

### Losing Stamina

When an effect requires you to spend **Stamina**, do so on your character sheet. When you spend your last **Stamina**, any further **Stamina** spent instead costs **Hit Points**. 

### Reducing Damage With Stamina

All characters can spend **Stamina** to reduce damage. When you take damage, you may spend 1 **Stamina** to reduce the damage by 1 damage threshold. You can only do this once per instance of damage.

### Regaining Stamina

Like Hit Points, you usually regain **Stamina** through **Resting** or a technique or spell that invigorates.

## Miracles

**Miracles** are a player-exclusive resource, representing how a twist of fate can massively benefit a character. All characters can only have 5 **Miracles** at once. If a character already has 5 **Miracles** and would gain another, they may immediately use the extra **Miracle** if they are able to, otherwise it is lost.

### Gaining a Miracle

You usually gain a **Miracle** by rolling a **critical success** or taking the **Prepare** rest action. Additionally, the GM may grant your character a **Miracle** when you play well into your character’s personality, do something extremely interesting, or give in to a drawback or flaw. 

### Using a Miracle

Every class has a feature that allows a character to utilize a **Miracle.** Otherwise, you can use a **Miracle** in any of the following ways:

#### Reroll a Check

You can use a **Miracle** to reroll your d20 on a **check**. You can also use this to allow an ally to reroll their **check** if you can feasibly support them.
<div class="break"></div>

When using a **Miracle** in this way, a **Miracle** can not be gained by rolling a **critical success** on the **check**.

#### In Place of Stamina

When an effect would require you to spend **Stamina**, instead use a **Miracle.**

#### Utilize a Miracle Feature

Some features require **Miracles** as a resource. Spend **Miracles** to utilize them.

## Doom

**Doom** is a GM-exclusive resource, representing how a twist of fate can severely hinder the player characters. At the start of a journey or campaign, the GM gains an amount of **Doom** equal to the number of players. You can hold up to a maximum of 10 **Doom**. If you already have 10 **Doom** and would gain more, it is lost.

### Gaining Doom

Whenever the players roll a **failure,** you gain **Doom.** If a player rolls a **critical failure**, you gain an additional **Doom**. You can also gain **Doom** from the players taking a **Rest**, certain player abilities, and enemy abilities.

### Using Doom

When you use **Doom**, describe how fate changes to counter the players. What new effect or element works against their characters? You can spend **Doom** for any of the following effects:

#### Taking the Focus

You can spend **Doom** to take the **Focus** from a player after one of their **Actions** and take an **Action** yourself.

#### Apply Disadvantage on a Check

When a player is making a **check** and has not already spent a **Miracle** to gain advantage, you may spend **Doom** to grant disadvantage.
<div class="break"></div>

You do not gain **Doom** from a failure on the **check**.

#### Utilize an Environment’s or an Enemy’s Doom Feature

Many environments and enemies have specific **Doom** features that signify powerful effects. Spend **Doom** to utilize them.

## Coin

Whether it be food, magical items, or a bed to sleep on, many goods and services require **coin** to purchase. There are no set prices for these goods and services, as it is up to the **GM** to determine their world’s economy.
<div class="break"></div>

**Coin** is separated into three types: copper, silver, and gold. Going up in value in that order. Ten copper equals 1 silver, and ten silver equals one gold.

## Skill Points

As mentioned in the **Creating a Character** section, you improve your character’s abilities through **Skill Points.** 

### Acquiring Skill Points

You gain Skill Points by reaching certain milestones on your journey. Maybe you conquered a dangerous dungeon, reached a long-term goal of the party, or completed a quest.
<div class="break"></div>

The number of Skill Points you gain is up to the GM, but every person in the party should gain Skill Points at the same rate.

# Death {#death}

All journeys must come to an end, and some characters meet an unfortunate one. However, the death of a character can be an exciting and emotional end to their story, as well as an opportunity for a player to try something new. When you lose all of your Hit Points, you immediately take a **death action**. A **death check** is a special check that has no associated **basic trait**.

## Death Actions

When you would take a **death actions**, choose from the following:

### Blaze of Glory

Your character accepts the end and goes out with a bang to aid their allies one last time. Take any **action** or describe how you sacrifice yourself. If the **action** would require a **check**, it is automatically a **critical success.** Afterwards, your character dies.

### Cling to Life

In an effort to stay alive, your character allows themself to fall unconscious. Your character can not move or take the **Focus** while unconscious, they can’t be targeted by an attack, and they return to consciousness when they regain Hit Points. When you regain consciousness, make a **death check**. On a **critical fail**, your character gains a **scar**.

### Make a Stand

In the hopes of fighting your way out, your character pushes through the pain to stay on their feet. Until you regain Hit Points, if you would lose Hit Points, make a **death check**. If you would take Major or greater damage, roll with disadvantage.  On a **critical fail**, your character dies.

## Scars

There are consequences to avoiding the pull of death. If you choose to **Cling to Life,** there is a chance to take a **scar**. If you do, describe how your character is affected, visually (if it is a physical injury) and/or mentally. Regardless of what form the **scar** takes, you lose one of your **Miracle** slots when you gain one. **Scars** are permanent, but the GM may allow you to regain the lost **Miracle** slot as a **long-term project** or as a reward for an adventure focused on the **scar**.
<div class="break"></div>

If you ever lose all of your **Miracle** slots, your character’s journey ends. While they do not necessarily die, they no longer have the motivation to continue adventuring and part ways with the party.

## Resurrection

When a character dies, there is no defined way to get them back, as that form of magic is currently unknown.
<div class="break"></div>

However, the GM may choose to give the party a chance to revive a dead character. The path is often a long, difficult, and expensive process.

# Equipment {#equipment}

This section describes the rules for equipment and items that characters can possess.

## Using Equipment

You **equip** weapons and armor to your character by recording them on your character sheet in the **Weapons** and **Armor** sections. Your character only benefits from equipped items.
<div class="break"></div>

Some equipment have features, which is detailed in their statblock.

## Equipping Weapons

You can equip up to two weapons at once. When you equip a weapon while already having two equipped, one must be sent back into your inventory.
<div class="break"></div>

When your character is in a dangerous situation, you can equip one weapon from your inventory as part of an **Action**.

## Equipping Armor

You can only equip one set of armor at once. When you equip a set of armor while have another equipped, one must be sent back into your inventory.
<div class="break"></div>

You can’t equip armor while in danger, instead needing a calm situation.

## Using Weapons

You can make an **Attack check** with any weapons you have equipped, using its unique statistics to determine how the **Attack check** is made.

### Weapon Statistics

Each weapon includes its name, trait, range, damage die, damage type, burden, and feature.

#### Name

The name of the weapon helps to identify its visuals and differentiate it from other weapons.

#### Trait

The trait tells you which **Basic Trait** is used when making an **Attack check**. You add your modifier of the **Basic Trait** to **Attack checks** and **damage rolls** of the weapon.

#### Range

The range tells you the maximum distance from which you can target a creature with the weapon.

#### Damage

The damage tells you what dice to roll and the type of damage you deal, physical and/or magical. 
<div class="break"></div>

Your **High Tier** determines the number of damage you roll.

#### Burden

The burden indicates if the weapon is one-handed or two-handed. A two-handed weapon means that you can not equip a second weapon, unless stated otherwise.

#### Feature

The feature is a specific ability tied to the weapon. Most features can be triggered when you make an attack with the weapon, and can not be triggered when using a different weapon.

## Using Armor

Armor usually has no defined actions related to it, but instead passively affects your character with its unique statistics.

### Armor Statistics

Each set of armor includes its name, damage threshold bonus, and feature.

#### Name

The name of the armor helps identify its visuals and differentiate it from other armor.

#### Damage Threshold Bonus

The Damage Threshold Bonus represents how protective the armor is when you are wearing it. You add the Damage Threshold Bonus to your character’s damage thresholds.

#### Feature

The feature is a specific ability tied to the armor.  Most features are passives and often affect your character’s Evasion.

## Unarmored

Wearing no armor forgoes any damage threshold bonuses you would gain from wearing armor. However, you gain a \+1 bonus to your Evasion.

# Spells and Techniques {#spells-and-techniques}

Spells and Techniques are special abilities that characters have, each having a specific use case and effect. Using **Spells** and **Techniques** costs **mana** and **stamina,** respectively.

## Learning New Spells and Techniques

As described in the **Creating a Character** section, you can learn new spells and techniques by spending **Skill Points**. The cost of learning a specific Spell or Technique is noted in its description.

### Inventing Spells and Techniques

Your character can create entirely new spells and techniques that do not already exist or change the function of an existing spell to fulfill a new use. When you do so, work with the GM to determine the exact effects of the new spell or technique, its **Augment Effects**, and the cost of learning it.
<div class="break"></div>

The GM may rule that your character must spend time as a **Long-term Project** to invent the spell or technique.

## Improving Spells and Techniques

As described in the **Creating a Character** section, you can upgrade spells and techniques by spending **Skill Points**. The cost of improving a specific Spell or Technique is equal to its **Tier**. The **Tier** of a spell or technique increases when you improve it.

## Using Spells and Techniques

Spells and Techniques are **Actions** that always cost **mana** and **stamina,** respectively. The amount of **mana** or **stamina** needed is noted in its description.

## Augmenting Spells and Techniques

**Augmenting** is when your character pours additional resources into a spell or technique to improve its effects.
<div class="break"></div>

**Augment Effects** are specific aspects of a spell or technique that can be **augmented**, usually the number of targets or damage. These are highlighted in their description.
<div class="break"></div>

For every additional point of **mana** or **stamina** you use on the spell or technique, you can choose a specific **Augment Effect** and double it. You can choose the same **Augment Effect** multiple times, but it only increases the multiplier instead of doubling again.
<div class="break"></div>

For specifically **augmenting** a range or area of effect, the range or area of effect is increased by 1 Tier for each time you **augment** it.
<div class="break"></div>

You can only **augment** a spell or technique a number of times equal to its **Tier**. 

### Augmenting Example

Requiem is casting  *Zoltraak* against a large crowd of enemies surrounding her. Her *Zoltraak* is currently a **Tier 2** spell, so she expends the **mana** cost and uses two additional **mana** to augment *Zoltraak’s* target count twice. Requiem wants to **augment** more, but she is limited by the spell’s **Tier**.  *Zoltraak* originally only has one target, so this allows her to target three enemies at once with this spell.
<div class="break"></div>

Nashir is using *Shockwave* to push enemies off a cliff. His *Shockwave* is currently a **Tier 4** technique, so he expends the **Stamina** cost and uses three additional **Stamina** to augment *Shockwave’s* area effect once, and the damage twice. Nashir is running low of **stamina**, so he doesn’t **augment** his technique to the maximum, and splits the **augments** to round out the technique.

## Activated Spells and Techniques

An activated spell or technique is one that you have prepared in your mind and body, ready to use at a moment’s notice. A deactivated spell or technique can not be used until it is activated.
<div class="break"></div>

You can have a limited number of Spells and Techniques activated at any one time. Your maximum number of active Spells and Techniques is equal to: **3 \+ your total Class Tiers**
<div class="break"></div>

Spells and Techniques share this limit. Any combination of active Spells and Techniques is allowed, provided their combined total does not exceed your maximum.

### Activating Spells and Techniques

You may activate a Spell or Technique by taking a Rest Action. Certain features, abilities, or items may allow you to activate them in other ways.

### Deactivating Spells and Techniques

If activating a Spell or Technique would cause you to exceed your maximum number of active Spells and Techniques, you must immediately choose one of your currently active Spells or Techniques and deactivate it.

# Companions {#companions}

Some abilities, spells, or techniques may summon or give you control over another creature as a **Companion**. **Companions** are often magical constructs or creatures that are either compelled or willing to follow your orders.

## Command

You may issue a command to a **Companion** to make them take an **Action**. You can issue commands to multiple **Companions** at once, but doing so requires a **Primary Trait** **check** determined by your **Command Difficulty**.
<div class="break"></div>

You may only command a number of **Companions** equal to your **Primary Trait** at once.

### 

## Command Difficulty

Each **Companion** has a **Difficulty**. When you command more than one **Companion** at the same time, the **Command Difficulty** equals 8 \+ the combined **Difficulties** of all **Companions** being commanded.

# Resting {#resting}

Journeys are long and often arduous, forcing your characters to frequently expend their resources to stay alive or further their goals. When the characters decide to have downtime, they choose between a **short rest** and a **long rest**, and then choose two **Rest Actions** to take.
<div class="break"></div>

While **Resting** is a time for characters to recover from the events of the day, it is also an important opportunity for them to have important scenes with each other. It is a time to learn more about their party, share a meal around the fire, or seek out a quiet moment with another.

## Short Rest

A **short rest** is when the characters stop to catch their breath, taking a break for about an hour. Each player chooses two of the following **Rest Actions** (or the same **Rest Action** twice):

* **Tend to Wounds:** You patch up yours or an ally’s wounds, regaining a number of **Hit Points** equal to 1d4 \+ your Hit Point’s **Tier**.  
* **Relax Your Body:** You allow yourself to relax and recuperate, regaining a number of **Stamina** equal to 1d4 \+ your Stamina’s **Tier**.  
* **Restore Your Mana:** Meditate on your magic, regaining a number of **Mana** equal to 1d4 \+ your Mana’s **Tier.**  
* **Activate Abilities:** You look over your spells and techniques, activating up to 2 of them.  
* **Prepare:** You and another ally prepare yourselves for the journey ahead, both gaining a **Miracle**. Both you and your ally must take the **Prepare** action.

You can only take two **short rests** before your next rest must be a **long rest.** If your party is interrupted during a **short rest**, you do not gain its benefits.

## Long Rest

A **long rest** is when the characters wind down for the day, sleeping and relaxing for about eight hours. Each player chooses two of the following moves (or the same move twice):

* **Tend to Wounds:** You patch up yours or an ally’s wounds, regaining all of your lost Hit Points.  
* **Relax Your Body:** You allow yourself to relax and recuperate, regaining all of your expended Stamina.  
* **Restore Your Mana:** Meditate on your magic, regaining all of your expended Mana.  
* **Spend Skill Points:** Train and study to improve your abilities, spending any **Skill Points** you have accumulated so far.  
* **Prepare:** You and another ally prepare yourselves for the journey ahead, both gaining a **Miracle**.  
* **Activate Abilities:** You look over your spells and techniques, activating any number of them, up to your maximum. Both you and your ally must take the **Prepare** action.  
* **Work on a Project:** Establish or continue work on a project (see the following “Long Term Project” section).

If your party is interrupted during a **long rest**, you only gain the benefits of a **short rest**, even if it is your third **short rest** in a row.

## Long Term Project

**Long Term Projects** are activities that a player wants to pursue that would take a substantial amount of time. This could involve deciphering a coded message, crafting a weapon or magic item, or something similar. Each project usually involves a progress counter, allowing the player and GM to keep track of how close they are to completion. Each time a character takes the **Work on a Project Rest Action**, they either automatically increase their counter or the GM may ask for a **check** to gauge their progress.

## Consequences of Resting

While your characters are taking downtime to recover, the world continues, and your adversaries move against you\!
<div class="break"></div>

On a **short rest**, the GM gains 1d4 **Doom**. On a **long rest**, they gain 1d4 \+ the number of players **Doom**, and may influence the world. They may describe a scene from outside the perspective of your characters or quietly change the circumstances.

# Conditions {#conditions}

Conditions are effects that alter a creature’s capabilities in a variety of ways, usually caused by a spell, technique, or extraneous circumstances. Conditions can be positive or negative, and sometimes both at once.
<div class="break"></div>

If the same Condition is applied by multiple sources, it does not stack its effects unless stated otherwise. 

## Clearing Conditions

Almost all Conditions are temporary, unless they are specifically denoted as permanent. A player character afflicted with a Condition can end it by making a **Basic Trait check** with a **DC** determined by the GM. You and the GM work together to describe how you attempt to and either fail or succeed at removing your Condition.
<div class="break"></div>

Multiple Conditions can be removed at once through a **check**, so long as it makes sense. However, the GM may adjust the **DC** accordingly to how many Conditions you are attempting to clear.
<div class="break"></div>

An NPC or enemy afflicted with a Condition can be ended as a GM Action, which does not require a **check**, but does usually mark the end of that creature’s turn.

# Additional Rules {#additional-rules}

Other rules to the game that don’t fit in one of the other categories.

## Rounding

If you ever need to round to the nearest whole number, round up unless otherwise specified.

## Rerolling Dice

If you are ever allowed to reroll a die, you always take the new result, unless stated otherwise.

## Simultaneous Effects

There is no order to how different effects should interact. The person causing or initiating the effects chooses which order they are applied. For instance, if you have a class feature that allows you to gain a **Miracle** on your first successful attack and another class feature that lets you deal additional damage by spending a **Miracle**, you could gain the **Miracle** first and then immediately spend it.
<div class="break"></div>

However, if you want to apply two or more effects, they must not contradict each other in the end. For example, if you regain **Stamina** when you **critically fail,** and you are also able to turn a **critical failure** into just a normal failure, you can not regain **Stamina** and then negate the **critical failure**.

## Affecting a Roll

Some features allow you to change the result of a roll after it has been totaled, such a using a **Miracle** to gain advantage on a **check**. In these cases, the feature can be used after knowing success or failure, but before another **Action** or dice roll is made.

## Duration of Effects

If a feature, ability, or effect does not specifically denote how long it lasts, then by default it should end when the scene it started in ends.
<div class="break"></div>

If a scene takes place for a very long time, then the GM may call for the effect to end during the scene.
